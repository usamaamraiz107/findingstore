Online shopping is convenient, and scammers know it. Fake stores, counterfeit products and too-good-to-be-true discounts appear on social media ads, marketplaces and even search results. The good news is that most scams share recognisable warning signs. Learning them takes a few minutes and can save you real money.

Red flag 1: a price far below everyone else

If ten reputable stores sell an item for about the same price and one unknown site sells it for 70% less, be suspicious. Genuine sales rarely undercut the whole market that dramatically, especially for branded electronics, perfumes, sneakers and designer bags. Scammers use extreme discounts to make people act before thinking. Comparing prices across many stores, which FindingStore does for you, makes these outliers easy to spot.

Red flag 2: pressure and urgency

Countdown timers that reset when you reload the page, "only 2 left" notices on every product, and messages telling you to pay within minutes are designed to rush you. Legitimate stores do run time-limited offers, but if the urgency feels manipulative, slow down and check the store first.

Red flag 3: a store with no real identity

Look for a physical address, a company name, a working phone number and clear policies for returns, shipping and privacy. Scam sites often have:

  • no contact details beyond a web form or a free email address;
  • policies copied from other sites, sometimes still naming another company;
  • a domain name registered very recently, or one that imitates a famous brand with small spelling changes;
  • spelling mistakes, mismatched currencies and product photos with other stores' watermarks.

Red flag 4: unusual payment methods

Be very careful when a seller asks for payment by direct bank transfer, mobile wallet transfer to a personal account, gift cards or cryptocurrency, especially if the website shows card logos but the checkout "fails" and they ask you to pay another way. These methods offer little or no way to get your money back. Prefer credit cards, trusted payment gateways, marketplace checkout or cash on delivery where available.

Red flag 5: reviews that don't add up

Hundreds of five-star reviews posted within a few days, reviews that are vague ("great product, fast shipping!") or that describe a different product, and no negative reviews at all suggest manipulation. Check reviews on independent sites and social media, not just on the store's own pages. Search for the store name plus words like "scam", "review" or "complaint".

Red flag 6: social media ads and fake pages

Many scams start with an eye-catching ad on social media. Some imitate well-known brands with copied logos and photos. Before buying from an ad, search for the brand's official website separately rather than clicking through, and compare the web address carefully.

How to verify a deal before paying

  1. Search the product elsewhere. Compare prices across several known stores. A huge outlier needs an explanation.
  2. Reverse search the product photo. If the same image appears on dozens of unrelated sites, the seller may not actually have the item.
  3. Check the domain. Look for HTTPS, the correct spelling, and how long the site has existed.
  4. Read the policies. Are returns, refunds and delivery times clearly stated?
  5. Contact the seller. Ask a specific question about the product. A real business usually answers clearly.
  6. Start small. If you are unsure about a new store, try a low-value order first.

Spotting counterfeit products

Counterfeits are not always sold by obvious scam sites; they also appear on large marketplaces. Warning signs include a branded item at a fraction of retail price, listings that avoid naming the brand directly ("inspired by", "premium quality AAA"), stock photos only, and sellers with many different brands at suspiciously low prices. For valuable branded items, buy from the brand's official store or authorised retailers.

If you think you have been scammed

  • Contact your bank or card issuer immediately to report the transaction and ask about a chargeback.
  • If you paid through a marketplace, open a dispute through its buyer protection process.
  • Keep screenshots of the listing, chat messages and payment receipts.
  • Report the site or account to the platform where you found it. In Pakistan, cybercrime complaints can be submitted to the FIA's cybercrime wing.
  • Change passwords if you entered them on a suspicious site, and watch your accounts for unusual activity.
