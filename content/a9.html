Discount codes, cashback offers, bank card deals and loyalty points can each knock a few percent off a purchase. Used together, carefully, they can add up to real savings. Used carelessly, they can tempt you into buying things you do not need or paying more than a simple competitor price. This guide explains how each type of saving works and how to combine them sensibly.

The main types of saving

Coupon and promo codes are entered at checkout to reduce the price, either by a percentage, a fixed amount, or free delivery. They are issued by stores, brands, marketplaces and partner websites.

Store vouchers are collected on the store's own site or app, often during sale events, and apply automatically or with a tap. Marketplaces frequently offer both platform vouchers and individual seller vouchers.

Cashback returns a percentage of what you spend after the purchase is confirmed. It can come from cashback websites, browser extensions, credit cards or digital wallets.

Bank and card offers give discounts or instalment plans when you pay with a particular bank card or wallet, common during big sales.

Loyalty points build up with each purchase and can be redeemed later as discounts.

Finding codes that actually work

Many coupon websites list expired or fake codes to attract clicks. More reliable sources include:

  • The store's own newsletter. Many give a first-order discount for signing up. Use a separate email address if you dislike promotional mail.
  • The store's app, which often has app-only vouchers.
  • The brand's official social media pages, especially around launches and sale events.
  • Your bank's or wallet's offers page.
  • Abandoned-cart reminders: some stores send a discount if you leave items in your cart while signed in.

Be careful with browser extensions that promise to find codes automatically. Some are useful, but they can see the pages you visit. Install only well-known ones and review their permissions.

Stacking: what can be combined

"Stacking" means using more than one saving on the same order. The usual rules are:

  • Most stores allow only one promo code per order, but platform vouchers and seller vouchers on marketplaces can often be combined.
  • Cashback from a cashback site usually works alongside a store code, but some cashback programmes exclude orders that use codes not published by them. Read the terms.
  • Card offers generally stack with store discounts, because they are funded by the bank.
  • Loyalty points can often be redeemed on top of other discounts.

A common sequence is: start at a cashback site, open the store through its link, apply the best store voucher or code, and pay with a card that has an active offer.

Watch the fine print

  • Minimum spend. A "500 off" voucher may require a 5,000 minimum order. Do not add things you do not need just to reach it, unless you were going to buy them anyway.
  • Maximum discount. "20% off" may be capped at a small fixed amount.
  • Excluded items. Electronics, sale items and certain brands are often excluded.
  • Cashback timing. Cashback may take weeks to confirm and can be cancelled if you return the item.
  • Price adjustments. Some stores raise prices slightly before a big sale so the discount looks bigger. Compare the final price, not the voucher value.

Compare the final price, not the discount

The most important rule: a discount is only useful if the final price is lower than what you could pay elsewhere. A store offering 15% off might still be more expensive than a competitor's regular price. Before applying codes, find the best plain price across several stores. A photo-based search such as FindingStore can show you local and international prices side by side. Then apply the savings available at the two or three cheapest stores and compare the final totals, including delivery.

Cashback and card rewards: hidden costs

Cashback and points are valuable only if you actually redeem them. Points that expire, or that can only be spent at one store, are worth less than their face value. Credit card rewards are never worth paying interest: if you carry a balance, the interest will quickly exceed any cashback earned. Instalment offers labelled "0%" sometimes include processing fees, so check the total amount repayable.

Avoiding coupon scams

Scammers use fake coupons to steal personal details or spread malware. Warning signs include messages promising huge discounts from well-known brands, links to sites with misspelled addresses, requests to share the offer with many contacts before you can claim it, and forms asking for card details or passwords to "activate" a coupon. Real vouchers are applied at the official checkout.

A simple savings routine

  1. Find the lowest regular total price across several trusted stores.
  2. Check each of the cheapest stores for vouchers in their app and newsletter.
  3. Check your bank and wallet offers.
  4. Go through a cashback site if one covers the store.
  5. Compare final totals and buy from the cheapest trustworthy option.

Summary

Coupons and cashback reward a little planning. Keep the focus on the final delivered price, read the conditions, avoid buying extra items just to unlock a voucher, and the savings will add up over the year.
