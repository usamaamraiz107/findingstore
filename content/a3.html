International stores can offer products that are hard to find locally, often at attractive prices. Buying from abroad also brings delivery delays, customs charges and harder returns. This guide covers what to check before ordering from an international store, with specific notes for shoppers in Pakistan. Rules and rates change, so always confirm current details with official sources before you rely on them.

Start with the total cost

The listed price is only one part of what you will pay. For an international order, add:

  • Shipping: cheap economy shipping can take weeks; express couriers are faster but cost more.
  • Customs duty and taxes: many countries, including Pakistan, may charge duty and sales tax on imported goods depending on the type and value of the item.
  • Courier handling or clearance fees: some carriers charge a fee for processing customs paperwork on your behalf.
  • Currency conversion and card fees: foreign-currency card transactions often carry a bank margin, and some banks add charges for international payments.

Comparing a local price with an international one only makes sense after adding these. A product that is 30% cheaper abroad can end up costing the same or more.

Understand shipping options

Stores usually offer a mix of postal and courier delivery. Postal services are often the cheapest but slower, with tracking that may stop updating once the parcel leaves the origin country. Courier services (DHL, FedEx, UPS, Aramex and others) are faster and track end to end, and they commonly handle customs clearance, sometimes billing you for duties before delivery. Check the store's estimated delivery window and whether tracking works all the way to your city.

Some shoppers use forwarding services: you get an address in another country, the store ships there, and the forwarder sends the parcel on. This can unlock stores that don't ship to Pakistan, but it adds fees and another party to trust. Read the forwarder's rules on restricted items carefully.

Customs in Pakistan: what to keep in mind

Imported parcels are subject to Pakistan Customs rules. Practical points:

  • Duties depend on the item and its declared value. Electronics, mobile phones and some branded goods can attract significant charges. Check the current rates with Pakistan Customs or the Federal Board of Revenue (FBR) before ordering expensive items.
  • Mobile phones need PTA registration. A phone bought abroad must be registered with the Pakistan Telecommunication Authority, usually with a tax payment, or it may be blocked from local networks.
  • Some items are restricted or prohibited. Certain medicines, weapons-like items, some chemicals and other goods may be refused. If in doubt, check before buying.
  • Keep your invoice. Customs may ask for proof of the value you paid. Save the order confirmation and payment receipt.
  • Never ask a seller to under-declare value. It is illegal, and it can lead to penalties or seizure. It also limits insurance claims if the parcel is lost.

Paying safely

Use payment methods that give you some protection. Credit cards and reputable marketplace payment systems usually let you dispute a charge if goods never arrive. Check that your card is enabled for international online transactions; many Pakistani banks require you to activate this separately. Avoid paying by direct bank transfer or cryptocurrency to unknown sellers, as those payments are almost impossible to recover.

Sizes, plugs and compatibility

Clothing sizes differ between the US, UK, EU and Asian brands, so compare the store's measurement chart with your own measurements rather than the label. For electronics, check voltage (Pakistan uses 230V, 50Hz), plug type, and whether the device is region-locked. Chargers and appliances designed only for 110V can be damaged without a converter.

Returns and warranties

Returning an item internationally is often expensive and slow, and some stores don't refund the original shipping. Read the return policy before ordering: who pays return shipping, how long you have, and whether opened items are accepted. International warranties may not be honoured by local service centres, so ask the brand's local representative if warranty support matters to you.

A pre-order checklist

  1. Does the store ship to your city, and with what tracking?
  2. What is the landed cost after shipping, duties and fees?
  3. Is the item allowed into Pakistan without special permission?
  4. Is your card enabled for international payments, and does it offer dispute protection?
  5. Have you checked sizes, voltage and compatibility?
  6. What happens if it arrives damaged or wrong?
