Most people compare prices by opening three tabs, glancing at the numbers and buying from whichever looks cheapest. That works for some purchases, but the price on the product page is rarely what you actually pay. Delivery, taxes, currency conversion, payment fees and return costs all shift the real number. Here is a practical method for finding the lowest total price for a product online, whether you shop locally or from abroad.

1. Pin down exactly what you are buying

Price comparison only works when you compare the same item. Before you search, write down the details that matter: brand, model number, size, colour, capacity, material, and the version or year. Two blenders that look identical can differ by motor wattage. Two pet beds that look the same in photos can be 50 cm or 80 cm across. Model numbers (often printed on the box, the label, or in the listing's specification table) are the most reliable identifier. When a product has no model number, as with many generic or unbranded goods, note the measurements and a few distinctive features so you can check every listing against them.

2. Search more than one way

A text search finds listings that use the same words you do. Sellers, though, describe the same item in very different ways, especially on marketplaces. Combine several approaches:

  • Text search with the model number in a general search engine and in shopping tabs.
  • Descriptive search with plain words ("round plush pet bed paw shape 60cm") when there is no model number.
  • Reverse image search, which finds visually similar listings even when the titles share no words with yours. This is what FindingStore uses: you upload a photo, and visual matches are grouped and sorted by price.
  • Marketplace search directly inside large local stores, because not every listing is indexed by search engines.

3. Calculate the landed cost, not the sticker price

The landed cost is everything you pay to have the item in your hands. A simple formula:

Landed cost = item price + shipping + import duties and taxes + payment or currency fees + any extra to return it if needed.

A $13 item with $18 shipping costs more than a $25 item with free delivery. International purchases can also attract customs duty and sales tax on arrival, and card payments in foreign currency often carry a conversion fee of a few percent. Add these up for each serious candidate; the ranking frequently changes.

4. Normalise currencies honestly

When you compare a price in US dollars with one in Pakistani rupees, use the rate your bank or card will actually apply, not the mid-market rate you see in a search result. Card issuers usually add a margin. If you are comparing many listings, a rough rule of thumb is to add 3–5% to foreign-currency prices to account for this, then check the exact figure on your statement terms.

5. Check the seller, not just the price

A very low price from an unknown seller is only a bargain if the item arrives. Look at the seller's rating, how long they have been selling, the number of reviews, and whether recent reviews mention late delivery, wrong items, or poor quality. On big marketplaces, prefer listings with buyer protection. Be wary when one listing is dramatically cheaper than every other, especially for branded goods; that pattern is common with counterfeits and scams.

6. Watch timing and price history

Prices move. Many stores run predictable sales around major holidays, end of season, and big shopping events such as 11.11, Black Friday and year-end sales. If the purchase is not urgent, note today's lowest landed cost and check again over a week or two. Some browser extensions and comparison sites show price history so you can tell whether a "sale" price is genuinely low or simply back to normal after an increase.

7. Use coupons and payment offers carefully

Store coupons, bank card discounts and cashback can beat a lower list price elsewhere. Read the conditions: minimum order values, caps on the discount, eligible cards, and whether the offer excludes sale items. Do not buy extra items only to reach a threshold unless you genuinely need them; that defeats the purpose.

8. Don't forget returns and warranty

A slightly higher price from a store with easy local returns and a valid warranty can be the cheaper choice overall, especially for electronics. Returning an item internationally can cost more than the item itself. Check who handles warranty claims in your country before you buy imported electronics.

A quick checklist

  1. Identify the exact product (model number or measurements).
  2. Search by text, by description and by image.
  3. Shortlist three to five reputable sellers.
  4. Work out the landed cost for each.
  5. Check seller ratings, return policy and warranty.
  6. Apply valid coupons or card offers.
  7. Buy, or wait for a sale if it is not urgent.

Tools like FindingStore speed up the searching and sorting steps, but always confirm the final price, delivery charges and stock on the store's own page before you pay. Prices shown by any comparison tool can be out of date by the time you click.
